Practice

Connect outcomes to ownership

When you assign work, make explicit what success looks like, not what activities should happen, but what result should be produced, by when.

For example

"I want the team to communicate better" is not accountable. "I want you to draft a proposed weekly update format and share it with me by Friday" is.

Practice

Create a regular feedback rhythm

Accountability tends to erode in the absence of structure. Short, regular check-ins, even a brief weekly written update, keep work visible and create natural moments to course-correct before things go off-track.

For example

Instead of finding out three weeks in that a project has stalled, a five-minute Friday written update (what moved, what's blocked, what's next) surfaces problems while they're still small enough to fix.

Practice

Separate feedback from evaluation

When something goes wrong, your goal is to understand what happened and fix it, not to assign blame. A debrief culture builds accountability toward outcomes and collective learning rather than individual protection.

For example

After a missed deadline, "why didn't this get done" invites a defensive answer. "What happened, what did we expect, what did we learn, what do we do differently" produces a fix instead.
