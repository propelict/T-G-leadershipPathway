Behavioural indicators

Observable, specific changes in how your team operates

Behavioural indicators describe what your team actually does, not how it feels. They're precise: what you observed, what you changed, and what happened as a result.

Behavioural indicators

For example

"Following the introduction of a structured weekly check-in, follow-through on agreed actions increased from roughly two of eight to five of eight over the sprint period."
"In the last two weeks, three blockers were flagged by team members before I asked, and all three were resolved within a day."
VS

Perceptual indicators

Changes in how your team describes their own experience

Gathered through check-ins, one-on-ones, or informal feedback. Softer than behavioural evidence, but still real data, as long as you're precise about what you heard and when.

Perceptual indicators

For example

"Three team members independently raised a concern about our meeting structure, which is what prompted me to change it."
"In one-on-ones this month, two team members mentioned they feel more comfortable pushing back on my proposals than they did last quarter."
